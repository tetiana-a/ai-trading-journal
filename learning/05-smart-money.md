# 05 — Smart Money Concepts

These notes organize the Smart Money concepts used during study and chart practice.

## POI — Point of Interest

A POI is an area where a large market participant may have shown meaningful interest.

It is a **zone to investigate**, not an automatic entry.

## Order Block

An order block is treated in study as a candle or price zone associated with strong displacement and significant order flow.

The useful question is:

> Did price leave this area with enough strength to make the zone meaningful?

## Liquidity

Practice includes identifying:

- single liquidity,
- liquidity pools,
- liquidity sweeps,
- equal highs/lows,
- stop clusters around obvious structure.

Liquidity concepts are most useful when combined with market structure.

## Imbalance

An imbalance is an inefficient price area created by strong directional movement.

It can be monitored for:

- partial fill,
- full fill,
- reaction,
- continuation.

## Market structure

Key observations:

- higher highs / higher lows,
- lower highs / lower lows,
- BOS — Break of Structure,
- CHOCH — Change of Character.

## Supply & Demand

### Demand
Area where price left aggressively upward.

### Supply
Area where price left aggressively downward.

A useful execution rule:

**Price reaches zone → wait for confirmation → consider trade.**

Not:

**Price reaches zone → enter immediately.**

Possible confirmations:

- liquidity sweep,
- strong rejection candle,
- BOS / CHOCH,
- lower-timeframe structure shift.

## Fibonacci

Fibonacci is used as a retracement framework, not as a prediction tool.

It helps identify areas to watch for confirmation.

## Wyckoff

Wyckoff study focuses on how price can move through accumulation, distribution, expansion, and re-accumulation/re-distribution phases.

Rather than memorizing labels, focus on:

- range behavior,
- failed breakouts,
- liquidity grabs,
- volume/effort versus result,
- transition from range to directional movement.

## Practice tasks

Good chart drills:

- find 4 order blocks,
- find 4 POIs,
- find liquidity sweeps,
- identify liquidity pools,
- mark imbalances,
- find demand/supply zones,
- identify BOS/CHOCH,
- compare Fibonacci retracements,
- annotate a Wyckoff-style range.
