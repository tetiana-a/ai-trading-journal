# 03 — Risk Management

Risk management is more important than finding more entry signals.

## Core rules

### 1. Never risk the entire account on one trade

Avoid oversized positions and emotionally driven leverage.

### 2. Always define the stop before entry

A stop loss is the point where the original trade idea is considered invalid.

### 3. Use small, predefined risk

A study example used in practice:

- Account: **$100**
- Risk per trade: **1–2%**
- Minimum planned R:R: **1:2**
- Preferred when the setup allows: **1:3**

These numbers are examples for learning, not universal rules.

### 4. Do not revenge trade

After a loss:

- do not immediately open another trade to recover money,
- identify what was wrong,
- separate a bad outcome from a bad process.

### 5. Control emotions

Common problems:

- greed,
- fear,
- urgency,
- overconfidence,
- hesitation.

If emotions are too strong, the correct trade can be **no trade**.

## Position planning

Before entry, define:

- entry price,
- stop loss,
- take profit,
- position size,
- maximum loss,
- expected reward,
- reason for entry,
- invalidation condition.

## Managing a winning trade

Possible management tools:

- partial profit taking,
- moving stop to break-even,
- trailing based on structure.

These should be planned, not improvised.

## Journal questions after a loss

- Was the setup valid?
- Was risk within the plan?
- Was the stop placed logically?
- Did I move the stop emotionally?
- Did I enter too early?
- Did I ignore higher-timeframe context?
- Was the loss acceptable according to the plan?
